Horizons, ATR units & risk-reward

Why ATR is the universal unit of stops and targets. The per-horizon bands we allow. The two flavours of risk-reward we compute. How we score predictions after the fact.

The four horizons we predict at

HorizonTrading daysWhat it means
DAILY1Next-session close (today's close if it's before 15:30 IST).
WEEKLY5+7 calendar days, snapped back to the last trading day.
BIWEEKLY10+14 calendar days, snapped back to the last trading day.
MONTHLY21+1 calendar month, snapped back to the last trading day.
Why both calendar and trading-day measures? The grading window uses trading-day counts (1, 5, 10, 21) — those match NIFTY's typical 21 trading days per month. The target evaluation moment uses calendar windows snapped back to the most recent NSE trading day — that way a prediction never silently extends past Diwali or a long weekend.

Why ATR is the unit of everything

A 1% move means very different things in different stocks. In sleepy HUL (typical daily ATR ~0.9%) a 1% move is a significant day; in volatile Vodafone-Idea (typical ATR ~5%) it's noise. So we express every stop and every target as a multiple of the stock's own ATR:

stop distance = (entry − stop) for bullish
target distance = (target − entry) for bullish

Then we ask: how does that distance compare to 1 day's worth of typical movement?

RELIANCE close ₹2,900 with ATR ₹40. A 2 × ATR stop sits ₹80 below the entry, or about 2.8%. A 1.5 × ATR target sits ₹60 above, or about 2.1%. Both numbers are "translated" into the stock's own volatility — the same 2× ATR stop applied to TCS would sit at a different percent of price.

The per-horizon ATR bands

The synthesizer is allowed to pick any stop/target distance inside the band for the chosen horizon. Numbers come from horizon_constants.py — the single source of truth in code.

Horizon Stop band (× ATR) Target band (× ATR) Entry zone (± % close) Conf. ceiling
DAILY0.5 – 1.00.75 – 1.5±0.5%0.90
WEEKLY0.7 – 1.51.0 – 2.0±1.0%0.85
BIWEEKLY1.0 – 2.01.5 – 3.0±1.5%0.80
MONTHLY1.5 – 2.52.0 – 4.0±2.0%0.75
Wider bands for longer horizons because longer horizons absorb more noise. A 1×ATR stop on a MONTHLY position would get eaten by routine wiggles every other week; a 1×ATR stop on a DAILY trade is appropriate.

Literature anchors. Wilder's (1978) canonical 1×ATR "swing stop" sits in the WEEKLY band. Van Tharp's (2007) 2–3×ATR positional stop sits in the MONTHLY band. The target midpoint of each row is ≈ 1.4× the stop midpoint — the positive-expectancy ratio Murphy / Pring / Tharp all converge on for retail timeframes.

Risk-reward — the two numbers we report

Every BULLISH/BEARISH prediction surfaces two RR fields, both computed. Why two? Because the textbook single-entry RR formula and the worst-case sizing decision are not the same number.

1. risk_reward — the worst-fill anchor

BULLISH: risk_reward = (target − zone_high) / (zone_high − stop)
BEARISH: risk_reward = (zone_low − target) / (stop − zone_low)
The "what if I got the worst possible fill inside the entry band" answer. This is what the conservative sizing rule reads: "even at the worst fill, RR is still acceptable". The guardrail enforces risk_reward ≥ 1.5 for any non-NEUTRAL prediction.

2. midpoint_rr — the textbook number

entry_mid = (zone_low + zone_high) / 2
midpoint_rr = (target − entry_mid) / (entry_mid − stop)    for bullish
This is the convention used in published edge studies (Bulkowski's pattern catalogues, Edwards & Magee, every broker's RR calculator). We surface it for literature comparability — not for sizing.

For any zone of nonzero width, midpoint_rr ≥ risk_reward (the midpoint fill is unambiguously better than the worst-end fill). They coincide when the zone collapses to a single price.

Grading thresholds (what counts as "right")

When we go back later to grade a prediction against what actually happened, we use a separate set of thresholds — the synthesizer was given an expected move band in ATR; the grader cares about actual realized return in percent.

Horizon Bars Mode-A win threshold Mode-A loss threshold Neutral cone
DAILY1+0.5%−0.5%±0.5%
WEEKLY5+1.5%−1.5%±1.5%
BIWEEKLY10+2.5%−2.5%±2.5%
MONTHLY21+5.0%−5.0%±5.0%
These thresholds follow approximately √(bars) × 0.5% — the random-walk scaling of how price drifts with time. A 5-bar week should see a ~1.1% standard deviation around zero; we set the bar a bit higher at 1.5% so we're only counting genuine directional moves.

Three grading modes

ModeQuestion answered
Mode A — strict directional Did the realized close-to-close return meet the horizon's win/loss threshold?
Mode B — target/stop resolution Walking the intra-window bars, which did price touch first — target or stop? Possible outcomes: TARGET_HIT, STOP_HIT, STOP_HIT_AMBIGUOUS (both touched same bar — counted as stop, conservatively), OPEN (neither touched by deadline).
Mode C — neutral cone For NEUTRAL calls only: did the realized return stay inside the cone?

Calibration metric: Brier score

How well does the agent's stated confidence match its actual hit rate? The Brier score is our answer:

Brier = mean((confidence − outcome)²)
where outcome = 1.0 if Mode-A correct, else 0.0

Lower is better. The naive baseline (predicting 0.5 confidence on a 50/50 outcome) is 0.25. Below 0.20 is "decent"; below 0.15 is "good". We additionally report the Brier skill score (BSS) — Brier compared to the empirical base rate, which is the apples-to-apples comparison if the base rate isn't 50%.
