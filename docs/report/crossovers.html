Crossovers — Golden, Death, EMA, MACD

Probably the most-asked-about set of signals in retail TA. We detect them rigorously, vote them conservatively, and report them honestly — including when nothing has happened.

What we report for every crossover pair

For each pair (e.g. SMA-50/200) we emit a five-field record on every bar — not just a "yes/no" when an event fires:

FieldWhat it tells you
current Is the short MA currently above or below the long MA? The "regime".
last_event The last time these two MAs crossed — was it bullish or bearish?
bars_since_event How many trading days ago was that cross? (3 = fresh, 47 = stale, 750 = ancient.)
short_ma / long_ma The two MA values at the latest bar — useful for "how close to a cross are we?"
Why all five fields and not just "cross today: yes/no"? Because a simple yes/no returns "no" 99% of the time, which is uninformative — and invites the LLM to infer a cross from the SMA stack alone (which is wrong). With this richer report the agent always has something true to say: "fresh cross today", "in-regime since 47 bars ago", or "no cross in 750 bars of available history".

The Golden Cross and the Death Cross (SMA-50 / SMA-200)

NameDefinitionImplied bias
Golden Cross SMA-50 crosses above SMA-200 on the latest bar. Bullish regime change
Death Cross SMA-50 crosses below SMA-200 on the latest bar. Bearish regime change
On the bar before yesterday, SMA-50 = 1,802 and SMA-200 = 1,810 → short below long. Yesterday SMA-50 = 1,808 and SMA-200 = 1,809 → still below. Today SMA-50 = 1,815 and SMA-200 = 1,810 → short now above long. That sign flip today = a Golden Cross with bars_since_event = 0.

Naming discipline

Only the canonical SMA-50/200 pair earns the marketing name "Golden Cross" / "Death Cross" in our agent's prose. Any other pair (EMA-9/21, custom user-requested pairs) gets generic phrasing like "bullish EMA-9/21 cross". This matches Murphy (1999) ch. 9 — the term is reserved for SMA-50/200.

⚠️ How much does the Golden Cross actually predict?

This is the part of the report that an honest practitioner pays attention to. The crossover signal is famous, but the academic record on its alpha is mixed and has weakened sharply over time on liquid large-caps. Three published studies you can look up:

StudyFinding
Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992), Journal of Finance 47(5) On the Dow 1897–1986, the 50/200 SMA crossover gave a statistically significant ~0.045% per buy-day excess return. Did not include transaction costs.
Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), Journal of Finance 54(5) Re-tested BLL using White's Reality Check to correct for testing thousands of rules. The 50/200 edge does not replicate out-of-sample (1987–1996).
Zakamulin (2014), J. Asset Management The 50/200 SMA-crossover Sharpe ratio drops from ~0.6 pre-1970 to ~0.1 post-1990 on US equity indices.
The honest takeaway. We ship the Golden / Death Cross primarily as a truth-telling, user-expectation feature — users ask the agent about it, the agent should answer accurately ("yes, fired 12 bars ago" / "no cross in 750 bars"). It is not a high-conviction directional signal on its own. The vote weights below reflect this conservative reading.

How crossovers vote in the trend verdict

The trend cluster's verdict is built from three things in order: the SMA stack (§Indicators), the ADX strength gate, and the DI confirmation. The crossover then acts as a nudge — it can push a neutral base verdict to bullish or bearish, but it cannot override a verdict already locked in by the stack + DI.

Vote rules

  • Only fresh crosses contribute — defined as bars_since_event ≤ 5. Stale crosses appear in the rationale only ("Golden Cross regime since 47 bars ago, stale; not voting").
  • Vote weights:
    • SMA-50/200 fresh cross: ±0.5
    • EMA-9/21 fresh cross: ±0.3 (faster pair = more whipsaws → lower weight)
    • Custom pairs: 0.0 by default (rationale only, no vote, until weights added)
  • The net vote magnitude must be ≥ 0.5 to nudge a neutral verdict.
  • The DI direction vetoes conflicting nudges. A bullish cross will not nudge to bullish if −DI > +DI.

Conflict by design

What happens when the two pairs disagree on the same bar — say SMA-50/200 bullish but EMA-9/21 bearish?

net vote = +0.5 + (−0.3) = +0.2 → below the 0.5 threshold → no nudge.
That's the right answer. SMA-bullish-but-EMA-bearish is the textbook fingerprint of a pullback within an uptrend — the long-term trend is up, the short-term swing is down. The agent reports both events but the verdict stays neutral, which protects against whipsaw entries.

The MACD cross (lives in the momentum cluster)

The MACD has its own crossover concept — between the MACD line and its signal line. Detection is the same idea as the MA crosses, but on the MACD histogram:

EventDetection on histogramMomentum vote
Bullish MACD cross Histogram was ≤ 0 yesterday, > 0 today. +1 bull vote
Bearish MACD cross Histogram was ≥ 0 yesterday, < 0 today. +1 bear vote

And if no cross fires today but the MACD line is still on one side of the signal:

  • MACD > signal (no cross today) → +0.5 bull — momentum still positive.
  • MACD < signal (no cross today) → +0.5 bear — momentum still negative.

The EMA-9/21 cross — the swing trader's faster cousin

Why also detect the EMA-9/21 pair? Because a lot of swing traders use it instead of SMA-50/200 — it triggers much sooner (often weeks before a Golden Cross does), at the cost of more whipsaws.

Same stock, same period. The SMA-50 might cross above the SMA-200 in early February. The EMA-9 typically crossed above the EMA-21 sometime in late November — three months earlier. The EMA cross caught the early move; the SMA cross confirmed the regime. Both are useful, for different reasons.

The EMA pair carries half the vote weight of the SMA pair (±0.3 vs. ±0.5) precisely because it whips around more.
