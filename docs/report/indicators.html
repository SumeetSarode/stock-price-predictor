Technical indicators

All four clusters: trend, momentum, volatility, and levels. Every number on this page is computed deterministically from the daily OHLCV bars — no LLM in the loop.

Note on lengths. All indicator lengths below use the standard sensitivity preset (the default). Two alternative presets exist: sensitive (faster lengths for day-trading) and smooth (slower lengths for positional). They only change the period parameters, not the formulas.

Cluster 1 — Trend

Simple Moving Average (SMA)

The plain old average of the last N closing prices. The most direct answer to "where has this stock been recently, on average?".

SMAN = (closet + closet-1 + … + closet-N+1) / N

We compute three lengths: 20, 50, 200 — short, medium, and long-term gauges (Murphy 1999, industry standard).

Derived: the "SMA stack"

Per SMA we record a boolean — "is the close above this SMA?" — and a signed percent-distance. The stack score is simply the count of SMAs the close is above, from 0 to 3:

Stack scoreMeaningTag
3Above all three SMAs — full bullish alignment.Bullish lean
2Above two; usually below the slowest one.Bullish lean
1Below two; only the fastest holds.Bearish lean
0Below all three — full bearish alignment.Bearish lean
TCS closed at ₹3,820. SMA20 = 3,780, SMA50 = 3,690, SMA200 = 3,560. Close above all three → stack score = 3 → bullish lean. The next gate (ADX) decides whether that lean becomes a real verdict.

Exponential Moving Average (EMA-20)

Same idea as SMA but the most recent bars get more weight. New bars carry weight 2 / (N+1); the previous EMA carries weight 1 − 2/(N+1).

EMAt = α × closet + (1 − α) × EMAt-1, where α = 2 / (N + 1)

Length used: 20 (Pring 2002). The EMA reacts to new prices faster than the SMA of the same length — that's both its strength (early signal) and its weakness (more whipsaws).

ADX and the Directional Indicators (Wilder 1978)

The ADX answers a different question than the SMAs: not where the trend is, but how strong it is. Plus and minus DI (the "Directional Indicators") tell you whether buyers or sellers have been in control of the trend.

ValueWhat it means
ADX < 20No trend Price is meandering. Treat any other signal with extra caution.
ADX 20–25The "grey zone" (StockCharts convention). Weak trend.
ADX 25–40Strong trend — Wilder's original threshold for "trend present" was 25.
ADX 40+Very strong trend. Often near exhaustion.
We use ADX as a strength gate. If ADX is below 20, the trend cluster's verdict is forced to neutral regardless of how bullish the SMA stack looks. A "bullish alignment in a directionless market" is just chop pretending to be a trend.

Convergence guard. ADX is doubly smoothed by Wilder's RMA (an exponentially-weighted average with α = 1/N). The starting bias only decays below 1% after about 10×N = 140 bars. We refuse to publish ADX before then, because an undercooked ADX value would mislead the strength gate.

Moving-average crossovers

We detect two canonical crossover pairs (SMA-50/200 and EMA-9/21) on every bar. Because the literature on this topic is rich and the implementation details matter, they have their own page. Quick preview:

  • Golden Cross = SMA-50 crosses above SMA-200 (bullish).
  • Death Cross = SMA-50 crosses below SMA-200 (bearish).
  • EMA-9/21 cross = a faster, swing-trader cousin.

Ichimoku Cloud (Hosoda 1969)

A five-line "one glance" system. On daily bars we use the canonical (9, 26, 52, 26) parameters:

Tenkan-sen (conversion, 9) = (9-bar high + 9-bar low) / 2
Kijun-sen (base, 26) = (26-bar high + 26-bar low) / 2
Senkou Span A (leading) = (Tenkan + Kijun) / 2, plotted 26 bars ahead
Senkou Span B (leading) = (52-bar high + 52-bar low) / 2, plotted 26 bars ahead
Chikou Span (lagging) = close, plotted 26 bars behind

The band between Senkou A and B is the cloud (kumo). We surface three signals from it:

SignalReading
Price vs. cloudAbove = bullish regime; below = bearish regime; inside = no-man's-land.
Tenkan / Kijun crossTenkan above Kijun = bullish momentum; below = bearish.
Kumo twistThe forward cloud flipping Senkou A above/below B — an early regime-change hint.
Role: unlike the SMA stack and ADX, Ichimoku casts no deterministic vote in the trend classifier. It is surfaced as structured data for the LLM to weigh — a rich regime snapshot, not a hard gate. Citations: Hosoda 1969; Patel, Trading with Ichimoku Clouds (Wiley 2010).

Cluster 2 — Momentum

Relative Strength Index (RSI-14)

Wilder's classic. Measures the ratio of up-moves to down-moves over 14 bars and normalises to a 0–100 scale.

RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS), where RS = average gain over N bars / average loss over N bars
ReadingWilder's classic interpretationTrend-RSI (Cardwell / Brown)
RSI > 70Overbought — may pull back.Still bullish in an uptrend.
RSI 60–70Strong, not overbought.+1 bull vote in our momentum classifier.
RSI 40–60Neutral middle ground.No vote.
RSI 30–40Weak.+1 bear vote.
RSI < 30Oversold — may bounce.Still bearish in a downtrend.
We deliberately use the 60/40 trend-RSI cuts (Andrew Cardwell, popularised by Constance Brown) for voting, not the 70/30 overbought/oversold flags. In an uptrend RSI oscillates between roughly 40 and 80 — using ">70 = bearish" in a strong uptrend would mean fading every single push. The 70/30 flags are still reported separately as regime labels for the LLM to quote.

MACD (Appel, late 1970s)

The Moving Average Convergence Divergence is built from three things:

MACD line = EMA12(close) − EMA26(close)
Signal line = EMA9(MACD line)
Histogram = MACD line − Signal line
SignalDetection ruleDirection
Bullish MACD cross Histogram flips from ≤ 0 to > 0 on the latest bar. +1 bull vote
Bearish MACD cross Histogram flips from ≥ 0 to < 0 on the latest bar. +1 bear vote
MACD > Signal (no cross today) Still above; consistent positive momentum. +0.5 in matching direction
Yesterday MACD histogram = −0.42. Today it closed at +0.18. That sign flip is a fresh bullish cross → counts as +1 in the momentum vote tally.

Stochastic Oscillator (Lane, 1950s)

Tells you where today's close sits inside the recent range. 0 = at the lowest low of the window, 100 = at the highest high.

%K = (close − lowN) / (highN − lowN) × 100, N = 14
%K is then smoothed over 3 bars (slow %K)
%D = 3-bar SMA of (smoothed %K)
ConditionVote
%K > %D AND both above 50+0.5 bull
%K < %D AND both below 50+0.5 bear
%K > 80Overbought flag (no vote flip; reported as label).
%K < 20Oversold flag (no vote flip; reported as label).

On-Balance Volume (Granville 1963)

A running cumulative sum: add today's volume if close went up, subtract if close went down, ignore if flat.

OBVt = OBVt-1 + (sign of close change) × volumet

The absolute level of OBV is meaningless — only the slope tells you something. We compute the 20-bar rate of change:

OBV slope (20) = (OBVtoday − OBV20 bars ago) / |OBV20 bars ago| × 100
Positive slope → +0.5 bull vote. Volume is confirming the price direction. Negative slope → +0.5 bear vote.

How the momentum vote becomes a verdict

Sum all four contributions (RSI ±1, MACD ±1 or ±0.5, Stoch ±0.5, OBV ±0.5). The signed total becomes the verdict; the absolute total scaled to min(|net| / 3, 0.85) becomes the classifier's confidence. Maximally aligned momentum (all four pointing the same way) caps around 0.83 — we never claim near-certainty from indicators alone.

Cluster 3 — Volatility

Average True Range (ATR-14)

The single most important number in the whole system. ATR is the unit in which we express every stop and every target.

True Ranget = max(high − low, |high − prev close|, |low − prev close|)
ATRt = Wilder smoothing of True Range over 14 bars

Why all three terms in the True Range max? The second and third handle gap risk — a stock that gaps open above yesterday's high has a true range that's bigger than its intra-day high–low spread.

RELIANCE closes at ₹2,900 with ATR = ₹40. That means the typical daily swing is about 1.4% of price. A 2× ATR stop sits ₹80 away, or ~2.8%. That's the unit we use for every risk decision in this stock.
ATR is reported as both a price (₹40) and a percent of close (1.4%). The percent feeds the volatility-regime label.

Bollinger Bands (Bollinger, early 1980s)

Middle band = SMA(close, 20)
Upper band = Middle + 2 × stdev(close, 20)
Lower band = Middle − 2 × stdev(close, 20)

Derived: %B

Where the close sits inside the band, normalised to 0–1:

%B = (close − lower) / (upper − lower)
%B valuePositionDirection vote
> 1.0Above upper band (breakout/overstretched)Bullish
0.55 – 1.0Upper half of bandBullish lean
0.45 – 0.55Around the middleNeutral
0 – 0.45Lower half of bandBearish lean
< 0Below lower bandBearish

Derived: bandwidth

bandwidth % = (upper − lower) / middle × 100

How "wide" the bands are right now, in percent of price. Used to detect the squeeze.

Two distinct "squeezes" (don't conflate them)

The TA literature uses "squeeze" for two genuinely different ideas. We surface both, separately.

Bollinger bandwidth squeeze
"Bands are quieter than usual"

Bandwidth is in the lowest 20% of its values over the past 60 bars (Bollinger 2001, ch. 11).

Role: diagnostic only. Surfaced in rationale text; does not drive any vote.

TTM Squeeze (Carter 2009) ⚡
"Spring is coiled"

Stricter AND-gate: upper Bollinger band sits inside the upper Keltner channel AND lower BB inside lower KC. Two volatility measures both compressed.

Role: actionable. When TTM fires (compression releases), the volatility classifier escalates strength to strong regardless of direction — breakout incoming.

The volatility classifier emits three fields

FieldValuesDriven by
Directionbullish / neutral / bearish%B above 0.55, around 0.50, below 0.45.
Strengthweak / moderate / strongStrong = TTM squeeze on/fired. Weak = ATR-% in dead-quiet (<1%) or manic (>6%) tail.
Regimelow / normal / highATR-% of price: <1% low, >4% high.

Cluster 4 — Levels

Levels are price points, not scores. They are the prices the synthesizer uses to anchor target and stop placement. Five families:

Swing high / swing low

The highest high and lowest low over the last 30 bars (≈ 6 trading weeks). The most recent actionable resistance / support.

52-week high / 52-week low

The highest high and lowest low over the last 252 trading days. The psychological levels every retail trader watches.

Classic floor-trader pivots

Computed from the most recent completed daily bar. Standard formulas:

Pivot Point (PP) = (high + low + close) / 3
R1 = 2 × PP − low  (first resistance)
S1 = 2 × PP − high (first support)
R2 = PP + (high − low)
S2 = PP − (high − low)
R3 = high + 2 × (PP − low)  (third resistance)
S3 = low − 2 × (high − PP) (third support)

VWAP — rolling and anchored

The Volume-Weighted Average Price treats each daily bar's "typical price" (the average of H, L, C) weighted by that bar's volume. Two flavours:

Typical price (TP) = (high + low + close) / 3
Rolling VWAP (20) = Σ(TP × volume, last 20 bars) / Σ(volume, last 20 bars)
Anchored VWAP = Σ(TP × volume, from anchor date) / Σ(volume, from anchor date)
VWAP acts as a dynamic support/resistance line. Many institutional desks target VWAP for their fills — so when price pulls back to VWAP in an uptrend it often attracts buying. The anchored variant lets us pin VWAP to a specific catalyst day (Budget, post-earnings gap, RBI policy) and watch it ever since.

"Distance to level" — the derived field

For every level above, we also compute the signed percent distance from current close: (level − close) / close × 100. This is what lets the classifier ask "is price near support?" without re-deriving anything.

The levels classifier verdict

Find the nearest level in ATR units (so it's regime-adjusted, not fixed percent), then:

  • Close within 0.5 ATR above nearest support → Bullish lean (holding support).
  • Close within 0.5 ATR below nearest resistance → Bearish lean (capped at resistance).
  • No level within 0.5 ATR → Neutral (mid-range, free to move).
HDFCBANK close ₹1,650 with ATR = ₹22. Swing-low support sits at ₹1,640. Distance = ₹10 = 0.45 × ATR → close is within 0.5 ATR above support → bullish lean, confidence ~0.6 (linear in proximity).
