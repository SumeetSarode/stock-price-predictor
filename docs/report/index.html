Prediction Walkthrough

How we make stock predictions

A complete, plain-English walkthrough of the data we read, the indicators we compute, the patterns we hunt for, and the way we combine all of it into a single, time-boxed call for one NSE-listed stock.

No code, no jargon-without-explanation. Aimed at someone who knows technical analysis and the news cycle, but does not read software.

📌 Five-line summary

  1. For one stock at a time, we ask: where is price likely to go over the next 1, 5, 10, and 21 trading days?
  2. We answer with five numbers per horizon: direction, entry zone, target, stop-loss, and confidence.
  3. The answer is built from two parallel reads: a technical view (chart math) and a news view (LLM reading actual headlines & filings).
  4. Both reads are passed to a synthesizer that weighs them differently per horizon (news matters more daily, trend matters more monthly) and writes the final prediction.
  5. Before shipping, every prediction passes four guardrail checks (grounding, citation, internal consistency, calibration). One retry, then abort.

The two-phase pipeline at a glance

Same indicators, same news read, same RSI value — fed into four parallel synthesizer calls that each produce one horizon's prediction. The synthesizer is the same LLM each time; only the prompt's horizon label, the per-horizon weights, and the per-horizon ATR bands change.

⚠️ A few honest caveats up front

  • This is educational output, not investment advice. Every prediction we ship sets not_advice = True and is_educational = True in the schema.
  • Many thresholds (the 60/40 trend-RSI cut, the 1.0/4.0% ATR-percent regime bands, the candlestick proximity gate) are literature-bracketed but not yet NSE-backtested. They are defensible, not empirically proven on your data.
  • The news layer reads GDELT, which is an English-language news graph. Vernacular Indian sources may underweight. Material moves can happen on a Marathi news leak that we never see.
  • We use daily bars only. Same-bar target/stop hits cannot be disambiguated; we conservatively count them as stop hits.
  • The corporate filings feed scrapes NSE's website (no public API). It is the most fragile part of the pipeline and can go silent for hours if NSE rotates their bot-detection rules.
