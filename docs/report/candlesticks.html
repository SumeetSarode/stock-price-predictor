A 90-second refresher on bar anatomy

Close above open → green/hollow body.

Close below open → red/filled body.

body = |close − open|
range = high − low
upper_shadow = high − max(open, close)
lower_shadow = min(open, close) − low

⚠️ The "context matters" filter (read this first)

Raw candlestick patterns fire constantly. A hammer in the middle of nowhere is meaningless. Trading wisdom — and Nison's whole book — says: a hammer only matters near support; a shooting star only matters near resistance. So before any pattern reaches the LLM, we apply this gate:

Pattern directionSurfaced only if…
Bullish The bar's low sits within 1 × ATR of the current swing-low.
Bearish The bar's high sits within 1 × ATR of the current swing-high.
Neutral (dojis, spinning tops) Near either swing extreme — indecision at any pivot is meaningful.

Patterns that don't meet the gate are dropped silently — the LLM never sees them. Every surviving pattern is enriched with context (near_support / near_resistance / near_either), the level_price it's near, and the distance_pct to that level.

Single-bar patterns

Indecision / continuation shapes (neutral direction)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
DojiOpen ≈ close. Body ≤ 10% of range. Pure indecision. Buyers and sellers fought to a tie. Meaningful near a pivot.
Long-legged dojiDoji with long upper and lower wicks. Violent indecision — large intraday range but flat close. Trend exhaustion signal.
Dragonfly dojiDoji with long lower wick, no upper wick. Sellers pushed price down hard but buyers rejected it back to open. Bullish-tinted near support.
Gravestone dojiDoji with long upper wick, no lower wick. Mirror of dragonfly. Buyers pushed up, sellers slammed it back. Bearish-tinted near resistance.
Rickshaw manLong-legged doji with body near range mid-point. Maximum indecision — wicks long, body centred.
Spinning topSmall body, both wicks visible. Tug of war. Weaker version of doji — same idea, slightly less symmetric.
High waveVery small body, very long wicks both sides. Extreme volatility/indecision. Often appears at trend turning points.

Bullish reversal shapes (after a downtrend)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Hammer Small body at top, lower shadow ≥ 2 × body, upper shadow tiny. Sellers drove price down all session, buyers absorbed and pushed close back near open. Classic bottoming signal at support.
Inverted hammer Small body at bottom, upper shadow ≥ 2 × body, lower shadow tiny. Buyers tried to rally but were pushed back; after a downtrend, signals buyer interest at the lows.
Takuri Dragonfly-like — even longer lower wick than hammer. Stronger version of dragonfly doji. Strong bullish rejection at the lows.

Bearish reversal shapes (after an uptrend)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Shooting star Small body at bottom, upper shadow ≥ 2 × body. Mirror of inverted hammer. Buyers pushed price up hard, sellers slammed it back to open. Classic topping signal at resistance.
Hanging man Same shape as hammer, but appearing after an uptrend. The same long lower wick that signalled a bottom now warns of weakness — sellers have showed up at the highs.

Conviction / continuation shapes

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Marubozu Long body, NO upper or lower shadows. Open = high (bearish) or open = low (bullish). Total domination by one side. Strong continuation signal.
Closing marubozu Marubozu where only the closing side touches its extreme; small wick on the opening side. Slightly weaker than full marubozu but same continuation read.
Long line Body is unusually long compared to recent bars. Strong directional conviction. Used by TA-Lib internally as a building block for many multi-bar patterns.
Short line Body is unusually short compared to recent bars. Pause / hesitation. Building block for harami-style patterns.
Belt hold Opens at an extreme (low for bullish, high for bearish) with no shadow on that side, closes far in the opposite direction. One side took control from the open and never let go.

Two-bar patterns

Reversal pairs (bullish)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Bullish engulfing Yesterday bearish; today bullish; today's body fully engulfs yesterday's. Buyers overwhelmed sellers in one session. Strong bullish reversal near support.
Piercing line Yesterday bearish long body; today bullish, opens below yesterday's low but closes above yesterday's midpoint. Weaker than full engulfing but same family — buyers reclaimed more than half of yesterday's range.
Bullish harami Yesterday bearish long body; today small body sitting inside yesterday's body. Selling pressure paused. Indecision after a downtrend. Reversal warning, not confirmation.
Harami cross Like harami but today's small body is a doji. Stronger harami signal — full indecision inside the prior body.
Homing pigeon Bullish harami where both bars are bearish (today's small bearish body inside yesterday's long bearish body). Bearish momentum decelerating — reversal hint.
Matching low Two consecutive bearish bars with identical lows. Sellers tested the same floor twice and failed both times. Support confirmed.

Reversal pairs (bearish)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Bearish engulfing Yesterday bullish; today bearish; today's body fully engulfs yesterday's. Sellers overwhelmed buyers. Mirror of bullish engulfing.
Dark cloud cover Yesterday bullish long body; today bearish, opens above yesterday's high but closes below yesterday's midpoint. Mirror of piercing. Sellers reclaimed more than half of yesterday's range.
Bearish harami Yesterday bullish long body; today small body inside yesterday's body. Buying pressure paused. Reversal warning at the top.

Continuation pairs (weak / strong)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Separating lines Two opposite-coloured bars open at the same price. The recent counter-move is rejected at the open of the new bar → continuation of prior trend.
Counterattack Two opposite-coloured bars closing at the same price. The counter-move reached parity at close — weaker reversal signal than engulfing.
In neck In a downtrend: bearish long body then bullish bar closing barely above prior close. Bounce attempt is weak. Continuation of downtrend likely.
On neck Like in-neck but the bullish bar closes exactly at prior low. Even weaker bounce. Strong downtrend continuation.
Thrusting Downtrend: bullish bar closes into prior body but below its midpoint. Bounce makes it halfway but no further. Bearish continuation.
Kicking Two opposite-coloured marubozu bars separated by a gap. Violent regime change — yesterday total bear domination, today total bull (or vice-versa).
Kicking by length Kicking where the longer body's direction wins. Same as kicking but emphasises which bar dominates by length.
Doji star Long body then a doji that gaps in the direction of the body. Indecision after a strong move — building block for the morning/evening doji stars below.
Hikkake An inside bar (harami) followed by a false breakout from the inside bar's range. Classic "trap" pattern (Linda Raschke). The false break sets up a reversal in the opposite direction.
Hikkake modified Hikkake with additional confirmation criteria (closer to Bulkowski's "Hikkake confirmed"). Stronger hikkake — only fires when the trap is decisively reversed.

Three-bar patterns

Reversal stars (the headline category)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Morning star Bar 1 bearish + long body. Bar 2 small body (any color), often gapping down. Bar 3 bullish + long body, closing above midpoint of bar 1. Three-act bullish reversal: capitulation → indecision → recovery. One of Nison's most cited patterns.
Morning doji star Same as morning star but bar 2 is specifically a doji. Stronger version — pure indecision at the bottom.
Evening star Mirror. Bar 1 bullish long, bar 2 small body, bar 3 bearish long closing below midpoint of bar 1. Three-act bearish reversal at the top.
Evening doji star Evening star with a doji as bar 2. Stronger version of evening star.
Abandoned baby Like morning/evening star but bar 2 is a doji that gaps away from both bar 1 and bar 3 (full price isolation). Rare and powerful reversal. The "abandoned" doji has no overlap with either neighbour.
Tri-star Three dojis in a row. Three sessions of total indecision — extreme exhaustion, reversal likely.

Strong continuation triplets

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Three white soldiers Three consecutive bullish long bars, each closing higher than the last, each opening within the prior body. Sustained, methodical buying. Strong bullish continuation or reversal from low base.
Three black crows Mirror. Three consecutive bearish long bars each closing lower. Sustained, methodical selling. Strong bearish.
Identical three crows Three black crows where each bar opens at the prior bar's close (no overlap). Even more aggressive selling than regular three crows.

Reversal hybrids (engulfing + one more bar)

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Three inside up / down Bullish/bearish harami with a confirming third bar in the harami's direction. Confirmed harami → stronger reversal call than harami alone.
Three outside up / down Engulfing pattern with a confirming third bar in the engulfing's direction. Confirmed engulfing.

Rare / specialised triplets

NameShapeWhat it tells you
Three stars in the south Three consecutive bearish bars with progressively smaller bodies and shrinking lows. Bearish momentum is exhausting. Rare bullish reversal warning.
Unique three river Hammer-like third bar after two bearish bars, with specific overlap rules. Bullish reversal at the bottom.
Two crows After uptrend: a bullish bar, then two bearish bars that progressively eat into the bullish one. Sellers showing up after a long run-up. Bearish.
Upside gap two crows Like two crows but with an upward gap between the bullish bar and the first crow. Stronger bearish — the gap reversal is particularly damning.
Advance block Three bullish bars that progressively shrink in body size and develop upper shadows. The rally is running out of steam. Bearish reversal warning during uptrend.
Stalled pattern Three bullish bars where the third has a noticeably smaller body and is "stalled" at the top. Buying exhaustion. Often precedes a pullback.
Breakaway Five-bar variant (TA-Lib uses 5 bars here despite the "three-bar" colloquial naming). A gap-led trend leg that ends with a strong reversal bar. The breakaway gap is filled and reversed — strong reversal call.
Ladder bottom Four bearish bars stepping down, then a fifth bullish bar that gaps up. Bullish reversal at the bottom of a stepped decline.
Stick sandwich Bearish — bullish — bearish, where the first and third close at the same price. Bullish reversal. The third bar "sandwiches" the bullish meat in the middle.
Tasuki gap Two same-coloured bars with a gap, then an opposite-coloured bar that partially fills the gap but doesn't close it. The original trend resumes — continuation pattern.
Gap side-by-side white Two bullish bars sitting side-by-side after an upward gap. Strong bullish continuation in an uptrend.

Four- and five-bar patterns

NameBarsWhat it tells you
Concealing baby swallow4 Two bearish marubozu, a third bearish bar that opens with a gap down and trades into the prior body, a fourth bearish bar engulfing the third. Rare. Strong bullish reversal at the bottom.
Mat hold5 A long bullish bar, then 3 small bearish/inside bars trading sideways without breaking the bullish bar's low, then a bullish bar that breaks out. Bullish continuation.
Rising / falling three methods5 Same idea as mat hold. Long bar in the direction of trend, three small counter-bars, then resumption. The "pause that refreshes."

Why we use TA-Lib's recognizers, not our own

Each of the patterns above is implemented inside TA-Lib's open-source C library (src/ta_func/ta_CDL*.c). Each function returns +100 for a fired bullish pattern, −100 for bearish, or 0 for no pattern. The recognizers were originally written by Mario Fortier and have been the de-facto reference implementation since the early 2000s.

We don't reinvent the math — we wrap their function calls in pretty names (hammer not CDLHAMMER) and add our own ATR-proximity gating layer on top. There's also a sanity check at startup: if a future TA-Lib release adds or removes a CDL* function, our test suite fails immediately so we can update the registry.
