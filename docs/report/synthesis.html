Putting it together

How the synthesizer turns four cluster verdicts plus a news read into a single prediction: direction, entry, target, stop, confidence. Runs once per horizon in parallel.

What the synthesizer sees on its desk

  • The TechnicalView: the four cluster verdicts + their underlying numbers (so it can quote exact RSI, ADX, %B values).
  • The ImpactAssessment: directional read, magnitude, confidence, and the up-to-5 catalysts and risks the news agent surfaced.
  • The horizon label: DAILY, WEEKLY, BIWEEKLY, or MONTHLY.
  • The current close price (anchors all the math).
  • The current ATR (the unit for stop and target distances).
  • The current timestamp (tz-aware IST).

What it deliberately does NOT see

  • The previous prediction for the same ticker (each call is independent).
  • Any backtest outcome from past predictions (no feedback loop).
  • The target_datetime (calendar math happens after, in a computed field).
  • The "other horizons'" outputs (they may disagree — that's information for the user).

How technicals vs. news are weighed, per horizon

The synthesizer is an LLM agent, not a formula. It does not apply fixed numeric weights to technicals and news. It reads both evidence streams and reasons over them; the horizon only decides the tie-break when the two point in opposite directions.

Disagreement tie-break

HorizonWhen technicals and news disagree…
DAILY / WEEKLYLean technical — price action wins near-term.
BIWEEKLY / MONTHLYLean news — catalysts dominate over multi-week windows.
Sharp disagreement
(e.g. trend bullish, news strongly bearish)		 Prefer NEUTRAL rather than picking a side.
Why this slope? Over a single day or week, the chart's own momentum and levels usually carry price. A multi-week window gives a catalyst — earnings, guidance, a policy change — time to actually reprice the stock. So near-term ties break to technicals, longer-term ties break to news.

Confidence is hard-capped per horizon

Longer horizons are inherently more uncertain (more events can intervene between the call and the target date), so the confidence the synthesizer may output is capped:

HorizonConfidence cap
DAILY0.90
WEEKLY0.85
BIWEEKLY0.80
MONTHLY0.75

These caps will be recalibrated from realized hit-rate once enough graded predictions accumulate (Phase 2).

Picking the direction

The synthesizer weighs the four cluster verdicts (trend, momentum, volatility, levels — each already carrying its own confidence) together with the news direction and confidence, and calls one of BULLISH, BEARISH, or NEUTRAL.

There is no fixed weighted-sum formula — the LLM reasons over the evidence under strict rules baked into its prompt:

  • It must state which clusters contributed to the call in its rationale.
  • It must surface any cluster that disagrees with the chosen direction as a conflicting signal (no quietly ignoring dissent).
  • It may not go BULLISH when 3 of 4 clusters are bearish and news is neutral — it must trust the weight of evidence.
  • On genuine conflict, NEUTRAL is the honest answer, not a coin-flip.

Placing the entry zone

The entry zone is a narrow symmetric ±% band around current close. The half-width depends on horizon:

HorizonEntry zone half-widthExample at close ₹1,000
DAILY±0.5%[₹995.00, ₹1,005.00] — total ₹10 wide
WEEKLY±1.0%[₹990.00, ₹1,010.00]
BIWEEKLY±1.5%[₹985.00, ₹1,015.00]
MONTHLY±2.0%[₹980.00, ₹1,020.00]
Tighter for daily (less leeway on entry timing), wider for monthly (more leeway). For a BULLISH call the band may be biased slightly upward (close at the lower edge); for BEARISH biased downward. For NEUTRAL it stays symmetric. The chosen worst-fill anchor (zone-high for longs, zone-low for shorts) is what the risk-reward math uses — see the Horizons & targets page.

Setting the profit target

The synthesizer follows this priority order:

  1. Nearest level beyond the entry zone in the predicted direction. That means: for a BULLISH call, find the closest level (swing-high, R1, R2, 52w-high) that sits above the entry zone — that's the target anchor. The rationale becomes "target=1605 — anchored to swing-high resistance".
  2. If no level exists within 4 × ATR, fall back to a pure ATR-based target inside the horizon's allowed band. The synthesizer picks a value in [stop_min × ATR, stop_max × ATR] and explains the choice.
  3. Whichever method was used, the synthesizer must state the rationale explicitly (e.g. "target=1623 — 1.5×ATR projection inside WEEKLY band, no clear level above").

Setting the stop-loss

  1. Nearest level on the OPPOSITE side of the entry zone. For a BULLISH call, that means the nearest support (swing-low, S1, 52w-low). The natural level is then verified against the horizon's stop ATR band — if it's too tight, push the stop out to stop_min × ATR; if it's too wide, the prediction must downgrade to NEUTRAL.
  2. If no opposite-side level exists, use a pure ATR stop at the midpoint of the horizon's stop band.
  3. Same explicit-rationale rule applies.

The risk-reward floor (1.5×)

After computing target and stop, the synthesizer verifies that the worst-fill risk-reward is ≥ 1.5. If it isn't:

  1. First, try moving the stop tighter (without leaving the horizon's stop ATR band).
  2. Otherwise, switch to NEUTRAL with a rationale explaining "no asymmetric setup at current price".

Forbidden: inflating the target to force RR ≥ 1.5. That would be the model lying to itself.

Computing the final confidence number

The synthesizer picks the confidence number itself — but honestly, and inside hard limits. It anchors the value to a plain-English band, then the per-horizon ceiling clamps it:

BandWhenRange
STRONGTechnicals AND news strongly agree; clear catalyst.near the cap
CLEARTechnicals lean clearly; news consistent / neutral-low-risk.upper-middle
LEANMixed; one strong signal partly offset by a counter-signal.~0.55–0.65
CONFLICTConflicting signals dominate → prefer NEUTRAL.0.30–0.50
GUESSGenuinely guessing → output NEUTRAL and say so.0.15–0.30
Whatever band applies, the number is then hard-capped by the horizon ceiling (0.90 daily · 0.85 weekly · 0.80 biweekly · 0.75 monthly). A "STRONG" monthly call therefore tops out at 0.75, not 0.85. The model is graded on calibration — confidence matching realized outcome — not on sounding confident.

When the synthesizer emits NEUTRAL

NEUTRAL is the honest escape hatch. The synthesizer must emit NEUTRAL when any of these are true:

  • Technicals and news sharply disagree (e.g. trend bullish, news strongly bearish).
  • The risk-reward floor of 1.5× cannot be met with any sane stop.
  • Price is mid-range — no nearby support OR resistance within 2 × ATR.
  • Volatility regime is "expanding" AND there's no clear directional catalyst.

NEUTRAL predictions still emit entry/target/stop fields, but they describe the expected range, not a directional trade. Risk-reward is set to exactly 1.0 by convention.

Citation & vocabulary discipline

Citation rule

Every claim in the rationale, contributing_signals, and conflicting_signals fields MUST be traceable to either:

  • A specific signal string in the TechnicalView (which the LLM may quote verbatim), or
  • A specific catalyst or risk in the ImpactAssessment (URL included).

The synthesizer is explicitly forbidden from inventing a "recent management commentary" that isn't in the news payload.

Forbidden vocabulary

The rationale must contain none of:

guarantee, certain, definitely, risk-free, easy money, multibagger

The output is educational, not advice — not_advice=True and is_educational=True are always set in every prediction object.
