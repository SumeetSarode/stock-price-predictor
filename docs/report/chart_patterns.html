Chart patterns — the five LMW shapes

Multi-week structural patterns: head & shoulders, double tops/bottoms, triangles, broadening, rectangles. Geometric tolerances from Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000).

Step 1 — how we find the swing pivots

Every chart pattern is built on top of swing pivots — local highs and lows in the price series. We use scipy.signal.find_peaks with a minimum separation of 5 bars between pivots. Anything closer than 5 bars isn't treated as a real pivot.

Honest deviation from LMW. The academic paper uses a Nadaraya–Watson kernel-regression smoother to identify pivots, then applies the geometric definitions to the smoothed series. We use the simpler peak-finder on raw bars instead. The trade-off: cheaper compute, no bandwidth-tuning knob, but slightly more sensitivity to single-bar wicks. The downstream tolerances (1.5%, 0.75%, 22-bar separation) are LMW's — only the pivot-finding step differs.

Step 2 — the confidence floor

Every detected pattern is scored on a 0–1 scale by how cleanly it fits the geometric definition. Anything below 0.7 is dropped silently. Below that floor the noise-to-signal ratio is too high to help.

Double top & double bottom

Geometric definition (LMW Def. 5)

  • Two consecutive swing highs (or lows) at similar prices.
  • The two peaks must be within 1.5% of their average price.
  • The two peaks must be at least 22 trading days apart (LMW's operationalisation of Edwards & Magee's "about a month" guidance).
  • A real trough (or peak, for double bottom) must exist between the two extremes.

Confidence formula

confidence = peak_similarity × depth_score

peak_similarity = 1.0 at identical peaks, falling linearly to 0.0 at the 1.5% edge
depth_score = min(trough_drop_pct / 5%, 1.0)

A trough that drops at least 5% below the average peak scores a 1.0 on depth.

Target projection

Double top target = neckline − (resistance − neckline)
Double bottom target = neckline + (neckline − support)
The "measured-move" projection: the breakdown/breakout is expected to travel the same distance as the pattern's height, measured from the neckline.
Two peaks at ₹1,510 and ₹1,498 (avg = ₹1,504, similarity = high). The trough between them sits at ₹1,420 (5.6% drop). Confidence ≈ 0.96 × 1.0 = 0.96 → easily passes the 0.7 floor. Target on a neckline break = 1,420 − (1,504 − 1,420) = ₹1,336.

Head & Shoulders + Inverse H&S

Geometric definition (LMW Def. 1)

  • Three consecutive swing highs where the middle (head) is taller than the two sides (shoulders).
  • Two shoulders within 1.5% of their average price.
  • The two neckline troughs (between shoulder1↔head and head↔shoulder2) within 1.5% of their average.
  • The head must be ≥ 3% above the higher shoulder (prominence requirement).

Confidence formula

confidence = shoulder_similarity × prominence_score × neckline_similarity
prominence_score = min(head_prominence_pct / 3%, 1.0)

Target projection

H&S target = neckline − (head − neckline)
Inverse H&S target = neckline + (neckline − head)

The inverse H&S is the mirror: three swing lows with the middle lowest. Same tolerances apply.

Triangles — ascending, descending, symmetric

Ascending (bullish bias)
Descending (bearish bias)
Symmetric (neutral)

Detection method

Fit a least-squares line through the last 5 swing highs (the upper trendline) and 5 swing lows (the lower trendline). Then classify:

TypeUpper lineLower line
AscendingFlat (≤ 0.75% spread)Rising (positive slope)
DescendingFalling (negative slope)Flat (≤ 0.75% spread)
SymmetricFallingRising

"Flat" definition (LMW Def. 4): the spread between the highest and lowest pivot price on that trendline is ≤ 0.75% of their mean.

Confidence

  • Baseline 0.7 for any matching geometry.
  • Symmetric triangles get a symmetry bonus: confidence rises toward 1.0 the more mirror-equal the two slopes are.
symmetry_ratio = min(|upper_slope|, |lower_slope|) / max(|upper_slope|, |lower_slope|)
symmetric_confidence = clamp(0.5 + 0.5 × symmetry_ratio, 0.5, 1.0)

Broadening tops / bottoms (megaphone)

Geometric definition (LMW Def. 2)

The mirror of a triangle — the upper line rises, the lower line falls. Pivots are getting more extreme over time, not converging.

Broadening patterns indicate rising volatility and emotional trading — often seen near market tops in retail-heavy stocks where each new swing is bigger than the last. Edwards & Magee (and our backtest backlog) treat them as bearish-biased reversal signals when they appear in an uptrend.

Rectangles (sideways consolidation)

Geometric definition (LMW Def. 4)

Both the upper and lower trendlines are flat (≤ 0.75% spread). Price oscillates between fixed support and fixed resistance.

Rectangles are continuation-or-reversal patterns — the direction is undetermined until the breakout. Our agent reports the rectangle and the breakout direction (when it fires) as separate signals; the synthesizer is not told to assume one over the other.

Patterns we deliberately don't implement

LMW (2000) excluded these from their canonical catalogue, and we follow suit:

  • Cup & handle — no closed-form geometric definition with published tolerances.
  • Flags, pennants — Bulkowski (2005) reports < 50% reliability on 5-year S&P backtests.
  • Wedges — overlap heavily with triangles and broadening; redundant given we already detect those families.
  • Elliott wave counts, harmonic patterns (Gartley, Bat, Butterfly) — require human pattern-completion judgement that a deterministic detector handles poorly.
  • Fibonacci retracements — useful as a derived overlay but require an anchor-point human decision. Tracked in the Phase-2 backlog.

If a domain expert tells us a missing pattern is table-stakes for NSE large-caps, we'll happily add it after a backtest.
